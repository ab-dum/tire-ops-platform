Case study · AI-built operations platform

AI on the shop floor

A tire distribution company ran its whole operation on a rigid ERP and spreadsheets. In six months I rebuilt its core systems on my own, using AI as the build partner, so pricing, stock and invoicing finally run as one platform the business owns. Here is how it works, screen by screen.

4,000+ products 8 sales channels 150 storage locations 10-person warehouse team
The business

A small distributor running on manual work

The company distributes tires across eight European markets. Its entire operation sat on a rigid third-party ERP and a pile of spreadsheets. Nobody could extend the software, so the daily work stayed slow and error-prone: pricing thousands of products by hand against an online marketplace, keying in deliveries, counting stock on paper, and producing invoices one by one.

There was no in-house software team and no vendor willing to build what the business actually needed, so the platform below was built from the inside, feature by feature, against the real daily workflow.

What I built

One platform, three jobs

A single in-house operations platform, built with AI coding assistants to move at a pace that normally needs a full team while every business rule stayed under tight control. It covers the three areas that hurt the most.

Pricing

Market-aware pricing

Live competitor offers feed a rule engine that reprices per brand, season and stock source, with review before anything goes live.

Inventory

Warehouse & stock

Barcode-scan counting across 150 locations, own stock and dropship suppliers, in a multi-language tool the floor team uses daily.

Invoicing

Invoicing & documents

Invoices and delivery notes generated automatically with correct VAT per country, replacing one-by-one manual entry.

01 · Pricing

Competitive pricing, one reviewed decision

Before this, someone opened the marketplace in a browser and compared prices tire by tire, then typed a new number back into the ERP. Across 4,000+ products and eight countries that was impossible to keep current, so prices drifted and margin leaked.

Now the platform pulls live competitor offers for each product, places our own cost and stock beside them, ranks where we would sit among sellers in each market, and proposes a new price from a rule set per brand and season. Nothing is published until it is reviewed, so the speed of automation keeps the safety of a human decision.

02 · Inventory

Stock counts by barcode, not by clipboard

The annual count used to take twenty people five days, on paper, with the warehouse half-frozen. Now the team scans an EAN or matchcode, picks the storage location, and the item lands with its DOT date and quantity. Sessions run independently, so several people count different aisles at the same time, and progress is tracked live across all 150 locations.

Because a tire carries a DOT production date, the scan captures that too, which later feeds pricing and stock decisions. The same interface runs in several languages for a mixed warehouse team.

03 · Invoicing

Documents that fill themselves in

Every order used to become a hand-typed document, which is slow and easy to get wrong, the VAT most of all. Now invoices and delivery notes are generated straight from the order: line items, quantities and prices filled in, numbered in sequence, with the correct VAT rule applied automatically (20% inside Austria, reverse charge for other EU business customers).

They come out as print-ready PDFs with one consistent layout, so documents leave the office the same way every time.

04 · Deliveries

Goods-in that updates stock and cost at once

When a supplier delivery arrives, it is booked in against the order instead of being counted and priced by hand. Each line raises stock and records the real purchase cost from the supplier's net price, not a catalog guess, so margins downstream stay honest. Partial deliveries are handled, and the same line is never booked twice.

05 · Range

Selling beyond the warehouse

The business does not only sell what sits on its own shelves. The platform ingests live stock-and-price feeds from several dropship suppliers, merges them into one catalog, applies a safety buffer to the quantity and a rule on tire age, and publishes selected items to the online shop. That widens the sellable range far beyond on-hand stock, without tying up cash in inventory.

How it's built

Under the hood

A lightweight web stack on a single server, so it stays cheap to host and simple to maintain. The pricing side continuously scrapes competitor offers and feeds a rank-based rule engine; an ETL layer ingests ERP exports, supplier catalogs and external market data into one database; and a review workflow exposes margin analytics before any price goes live. The guiding rule throughout: tools that non-technical staff can trust and run themselves.
