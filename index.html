AI on the shop floor

A tire distribution company ran its whole operation on a rigid ERP and spreadsheets. Over two years I rebuilt its core systems on my own, using AI as the build partner, so pricing, stock and invoicing finally run as one platform the business owns. Here is how it works.

4,000+ products 8 sales channels 150 storage locations 10-person warehouse team
The business

A small distributor running on manual work

The company distributes tires across eight European markets. Its entire operation sat on a rigid third-party ERP and a pile of spreadsheets. Nobody could extend the software, so the daily work stayed slow and error-prone: pricing thousands of products by hand against an online marketplace, keying in deliveries, counting stock on paper, and producing invoices one by one.

There was no in-house software team and no vendor willing to build what the business actually needed, so the system below was built from the inside.

What I built

One in-house platform, three jobs

The result is a single in-house operations platform, built with AI coding assistants to move at a pace that normally needs a full team while every business rule stayed under tight control. It covers the three areas that hurt the most.

Pricing

Market-aware pricing

Pulls live competitor offers from an online marketplace and applies configurable rules per brand, season and stock source, with a review step before anything goes live.

Inventory

Warehouse & stock

Barcode-scan stock counting across 150 locations and our own plus dropship suppliers, in a multi-language tool the floor team actually uses.

Invoicing

Invoicing & documents

Generates invoices and delivery documents automatically with correct VAT per country, replacing one-by-one manual entry.

How it's built

Inside the platform

A lightweight web stack running on a single server, so it stays cheap to host and simple to maintain. The market-pricing side continuously scrapes competitor offers and feeds a rank-based rule engine; an ETL layer ingests ERP exports, supplier catalogs and external market data into one database; and a review workflow shows margin analytics before any price goes live. The point throughout is tools that non-technical staff can trust and run themselves.
